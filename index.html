Privacy First & Always

Local-First Privacy, Explained

Kunjika keeps its vault on your device and does not provide an account or cloud-sync service. Here is what that means in practice.

No Direct App Internet Permission

The Android app manifest removes android.permission.INTERNET. Kunjika has no account or cloud-sync service. It can request a Play Integrity token through Google Play services, and users can share selected data through Bluetooth, QR, clipboard, Autofill, or exported files.

No Analytics Service

Kunjika does not include third-party analytics or crash-reporting SDKs. Device-level services have their own privacy practices.

No Kunjika Account or Cloud Sync

You do not need a Kunjika account. Kunjika does not operate a cloud database or sync service. Keep an encrypted backup if you need to restore your vault.

Encrypted Local Vault

The vault database is protected by SQLCipher. Password and TOTP-secret fields also use AES-GCM. Other metadata is protected by database encryption, and hardware backing varies by device.

Transparent Breakdown of Device Permissions

USE_BIOMETRIC Allows biometric approval for vault access and Keystore operations on supported devices. Hardware backing depends on your device. The PIN may exist temporarily in app memory during authentication.
CAMERA Used to scan QR codes for transfers and authenticator setup. Kunjika does not save scanned frames as photos.
BLUETOOTH_ADVERTISE / CONNECT Used during a user-initiated Web Drop session to transfer encrypted credential data to a nearby browser. Advertising ends when the session ends.
Storage Access Framework Kunjika does not request broad file access. When you choose to export an encrypted .kunjika backup, it uses the official Android system file picker (SAF) to let you pick a USB drive, SD card, or local folder.

Clear & Grounded Security Boundaries

• Hardware Backing: Keystore hardware protection depends on your device.
• Access Gate: The Master PIN gates app access; it is not the database key. The SQLCipher passphrase is random and wrapped by Android Keystore.
• Sharing: Web Drop, QR transfer, clipboard, Autofill, and export move selected data into another app, browser, file, or device. • Browser: The hosted Web Companion page is mutable and is not independently authenticated. Use a trusted computer and browser.

Storage Choices

A Local-First Approach

Kunjika is designed around local storage and user-initiated sharing. This brings control over transfers and backups, along with responsibility for keeping your device and recovery copies safe.

Trade-Offs to Consider

  • Different Storage Models

    Cloud-sync services and local-first apps make different trade-offs for device access, synchronization, and recovery. Choose the model that fits how you use your devices.

  • Different Pricing and Features

    Plans and features vary across password managers. Check each product's current offering and pricing before choosing.

  • Receiving Credentials on a Computer

    Web Drop requires a compatible browser, Bluetooth support, and trust in the computer receiving the credential.

  • Authenticator Separation

    Keeping a password and its TOTP secret in one app is convenient; using a separate authenticator can keep the factors more separate.

Kunjika's Local-First Design

  • Local-First by Design

    The app manifest removes android.permission.INTERNET and Kunjika does not provide cloud sync. Google Play services may handle an optional Play Integrity request; users can also share selected data through system integrations.

  • Encrypted Local Vault

    The SQLCipher database uses a random passphrase wrapped by Android Keystore. Password and TOTP-secret fields also use AES-GCM. Keystore hardware backing varies by device.

  • Web Drop over Bluetooth

    Transfer a selected credential to a compatible browser after comparing the session codes and approving the send. The browser does not automatically copy or clear the clipboard.

  • Integrated TOTP Authenticator

    Store a TOTP secret with a vault entry and view its rotating code. Keeping both factors in one app can be convenient; a separate authenticator offers more separation.

Quickstart Guide

Get Started in 6 Simple Steps

No Kunjika account or cloud sync is required. Setup time depends on your device and choices.

1

Install from Google Play

Download Kunjika from Google Play on Android 8.0 or newer.

Direct link: Google Play Store
2

Set a Master PIN

Choose a Master PIN of at least six digits. You can enable biometric unlock on supported devices.

Tip: Biometrics are optional and device support varies.
3

Add Passwords & 2FA Codes

Use the 4-in-1 generator to create high-entropy passwords, or add existing logins with titles, usernames, notes, and 2FA authenticator secret keys.

Tip: Scan 2FA QR codes directly with your camera to start rotating 30s TOTP codes.
4

Enable Android Autofill

Go to your phone's Settings → Passwords & Autofill → Preferred service and select Kunjika for 1-tap logins in Chrome and apps.

Tip: Android may ask you to authenticate before providing a credential.
5

Try Web Drop to a Computer

Load the hosted Web Companion in a compatible browser, then scan its session QR with Kunjika. Authenticate on your phone, compare the six-digit codes, confirm in the browser, and approve the send on your phone.

Tip: Copy is user-initiated. Clipboard managers may retain copied credentials, and browser memory cleanup is best-effort.
6

Export Encrypted Backup

Go to Settings → Encrypted Offline Backup → Save File. Set a backup passphrase and choose a destination in Android's file picker.

Tip: Keep the backup file and its passphrase safe and separate.

User-Initiated Transfer

Kunjika Web Drop: Bluetooth Transfer to a Browser

After you load the hosted companion page, Web Drop transfers a selected credential directly from your phone to a compatible browser over Bluetooth. There is no cloud relay in the transfer path.

1

Open Web Companion

Open the hosted Web Companion on a trusted computer using a browser that supports Web Bluetooth. The page is mutable and is not independently authenticated. It displays a session QR code.

2

Scan & Confirm SAS

Scan the session QR with Kunjika. After you authenticate on the phone, both screens display a six-digit comparison code. Compare the codes before confirming in the browser and approving the send on the phone.

3

Approve and Receive

Approve the transfer on your phone. The browser decrypts and displays the selected credential. Copy it only when needed; browser memory cleanup is best-effort, and clipboard history may retain copied values.

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Learn how Kunjika's local-first model, security features, and setup work.

What if I lose or replace my phone? ▼
Create an encrypted vault backup protected by a passphrase and store the file and passphrase safely and separately. Use the backup to restore your vault on another device. The local database recovery kit is for recovering that device's database and is not a replacement for a portable encrypted backup.
How do I save my backup file to a USB flash drive or SD card? ▼
Go to Settings → Encrypted Offline Backup → Save File. Android's system file picker lets you select a destination. Keep the encrypted backup and its passphrase safe and separate.
Do I need both a fingerprint and Master PIN to open the vault? ▼
No. The Master PIN is the app's access gate. Biometric unlock is optional on supported devices. Biometrics do not make a compromised device safe, and the PIN may exist temporarily in app memory during authentication.
What are "High-Security Entries" and how do they protect my sensitive accounts? ▼
You can mark selected entries as High Security. Depending on the action and device support, Kunjika may request biometric re-verification before revealing or copying a credential. This is an app-level check, not protection from a compromised operating system.
What is the Anti-Coercion Duress PIN and how does the Decoy Vault work? ▼
The optional Duress PIN opens a decoy view with dummy entries. It is an app-level feature and cannot guarantee protection from a person with device access or a compromised operating system.
How does Web Drop transfer a credential to my computer? ▼
After you load the hosted companion page, Web Drop uses Bluetooth Low Energy to transfer a selected credential directly to the browser, without a cloud relay in the transfer path. The session uses ephemeral P-256 ECDH, HKDF-SHA-256, AES-GCM, and a six-digit comparison code. Compare the code, confirm in the browser, and approve on the phone. The hosted page is mutable and the receiving computer must be trusted. Browser memory cleanup is best-effort.
Can Kunjika replace Google Authenticator? ▼
Kunjika can store a TOTP secret with a vault entry and display rotating six-digit codes. This can be convenient; if you prefer more separation between factors, use a separate authenticator.
What does the missing INTERNET permission mean? ▼
The Android app manifest removes android.permission.INTERNET, and Kunjika does not provide cloud accounts or synchronization. The app can request a Play Integrity token through Google Play services. Users can also share selected data through Bluetooth, QR, clipboard, Autofill, and exported files. Review the privacy policy for details and limitations.
Is Kunjika completely free? ▼
Check the current Google Play listing for availability and pricing. The project source is MIT-licensed.
Where can I ask questions or report a bug? ▼
We welcome all questions, bug reports, and feature suggestions on our official GitHub repository! You can file an issue on GitHub Issues, start a conversation on GitHub Discussions, or email the developer at winsimash@gmail.com.
